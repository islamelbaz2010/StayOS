## Purpose

This article defines how StayOS retains both hosts and guests across the full lifecycle — from first activation through long-term loyalty. Retention is the single most important metric for marketplace unit economics: a host or guest acquired once but retained for years generates compounding value; a platform with high churn spends all its energy refilling a leaky bucket.

---

## Background

In two-sided marketplaces, retention problems are often asymmetric: hosts churn for different reasons than guests, and the fix for one side doesn't help the other. StayOS must operate two parallel retention programs — one for supply, one for demand.

**Key retention thresholds**:
- Host: 90% active hosting rate (any booking activity in 60 days) is the target
- Guest: ≥20% of guests booking again within 3 months is Stage 1 target
- Days to First Booking: ≤21 days after listing goes live (hosts)
- Host 30-day churn prediction: >30 days with no booking = 95% churn probability

Egypt-specific context: word-of-mouth and personal recommendation have significantly more weight than in Western markets. A retained, satisfied host or guest is also a referral source. Retention and acquisition are the same activity.

## Part 1: Host Retention

### Host Churn Signals (Early Warning Indicators)

Monitor every host for these signals. Immediate intervention is triggered by any of the following:

| Signal | Threshold | Action |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| Days since last booking | >21 days | Personal outreach within 24 hours |
| Calendar blocked rate | >40% of next 30 days blocked | Investigate: is the host deactivating? |
| Response rate drop | <80% response to guest inquiries | Personal check-in call |
| Listing view → booking conversion | <3% for 2 consecutive weeks | Pricing or listing quality review |
| Review score drop | <4.0 average for last 3 reviews | Performance conversation |
| Support complaint by host | Any complaint without resolution | Escalate immediately |
| Direct message: "I'm thinking of leaving" | Any occurrence | Escalate to founder within 1 hour |

---

### Host Retention Interventions

**Intervention Level 1: Regular Engagement (All Active Hosts)**
- Weekly "property performance summary" WhatsApp message: views, inquiries, bookings, and earnings this week vs. last week
- Monthly check-in call with a Host Success team member (scripted, 15 minutes)
- Monthly market update: "Here's what properties like yours are earning in [neighborhood]"

**Intervention Level 2: At-Risk Host (21+ Days No Booking)**

Day 21 trigger: Host Success calls (not WhatsApps) the host within 24 hours.

Script opening:
> "أهلاً [Name]، أنا [Agent] من فريق نجاح المضيفين في StayOS. بشوف إن الوحدة [Property] معندهاش حجز من [X] أيام وعايز أتطمن إن كل حاجة تمام وأشوف نقدر نعمل إيه مع بعض."

Investigate:
- Is the calendar blocked? Why? (owner use, renovation, or de facto withdrawal?)
- Is the pricing competitive? (check comparable listings)
- Is the listing description and photos accurate and strong?
- Has anything changed at the property?

Take action immediately in the call:
- Adjust pricing suggestion (5–10% below market if overpriced)
- Offer to update listing photos (schedule a free refresh visit)
- Review and update listing description
- Unlock any unnecessarily blocked calendar dates

Follow up in 7 days: "Did we see any movement after those changes?"

**Intervention Level 3: Host Signaling Intent to Leave**

Any signal that a host is considering leaving (including silence after the Level 2 call) triggers:
- Escalate to Founder within 1 hour if host has ≥3 properties
- Escalate to Host Success Lead for 1–2 property hosts
- Personal call from the senior person within 24 hours
- Offer: waived commission for next 2 bookings (approved by Founder)
- Question to ask: "What would need to be true for you to stay?" — then solve for that specifically

---

### Host Loyalty Program (Stage 1)

**Host of the Month**:
- Selected monthly based on: highest review score, most bookings, or biggest improvement
- Recognition: featured on StayOS marketing materials (with host consent)
- Reward: EGP 500 credit or commission waiver for 1 booking (host's choice)
- Announced via WhatsApp broadcast to all hosts

**Host Referral Program**:
- Existing host refers a new host
- New host completes onboarding and gets their first booking
- Referring host receives: EGP 500 credit applied to their next payout
- No cap on referrals (a host who refers 10 hosts earns EGP 5,000 in credits)

**Superhost Status (Stage 2 Target)**:
- Requirements: ≥90% response rate, ≥4.5 average review, ≥10 completed bookings, <2% cancellation rate
- Benefits: Priority listing placement, Superhost badge on listing, dedicated support line
- Review quarterly, revoke if standards drop below threshold

## Part 2: Guest Retention

### Guest Churn Signals

| Signal | Threshold | Action |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| No booking after first stay | >45 days | Win-back campaign |
| Booking started but not completed (cart abandonment) | Any occurrence | Abandonment follow-up within 24 hours |
| Post-stay review score ≤3 | Any 3-star or below review | Personal call within 24 hours |
| Support ticket resolved but guest expressed dissatisfaction | Any such ticket | Personal follow-up call within 48 hours |
| No second booking after 90 days | All first-time guests at 90 days | Win-back offer |

### Guest Retention Interventions

**Intervention Level 1: Post-Stay Engagement (All Guests)**

Day of checkout: Review request sent (Template D2 from communication_templates.md)

Day 3 after checkout: Optional check-in if no review received:
> "أهلاً [Name]! تمنينا تكون استمتعت بإقامتك معنا. لو عندك أي تعليق أو فكرة تحسن تجربة StayOS، سماعتنا كلها لك."
> "Hi [Name]! We hope you enjoyed your stay with us. If you have any feedback or ideas to improve StayOS, we're all ears."

Day 30: Seasonal property availability message targeting the guest's known preferences.

**Intervention Level 2: First-Time Guest at Day 45 (No Return Booking)**

Personalized win-back message:
> "مرحبا [Name]! عندنا وحدات جديدة في [المنطقة اللي أقام فيها] ومناطق تانية قريبة. لو بتفكر في رحلة جديدة، عندنا كود خصم [X]% للضيوف اللي بيرجعوا: [CODE]."

**Intervention Level 3: Dissatisfied Guest (≤3 Star Review or Complaint)**

Personal phone call from Guest Success team member within 24 hours:
- Not WhatsApp — phone call signals seriousness
- Opening: "أنا [Name] من فريق StayOS. شفت تجربتك معنا ومش راضيين عنها وعايزين نفهم أكتر."
- Goal: Understand the root cause, not just apologize
- Outcome: Meaningful recovery (credit, personalized property recommendation, direct booking assistance for next stay)
- No recovery is valid without understanding what went wrong

### Guest Loyalty Design (Stage 1)

**Repeat Guest Acknowledgment**:
- Second booking: "مرحبا بك مجدداً في StayOS!" — personal message from the Host Success team
- Third booking: Bonus EGP 150 credit applied automatically
- Fifth booking: Superguest status (early access to new properties, priority support)

**Referral Program**:
- Guest refers a friend who completes their first booking
- Referring guest receives: EGP 200 credit
- New guest receives: EGP 100 credit on first booking
- Maximum 5 referral credits per year per guest (to prevent abuse)

**Birthday Recognition (if date known from KYC)**:
- Send a personalized birthday message with a booking discount (10% off next stay, valid 30 days)
- Low cost, high emotional impact when received

## Part 3: Retention Analytics

### Metrics to Track Weekly

| Metric | Target | Alert Threshold |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| Host active rate (any booking in 60 days) | ≥90% | <85% |
| Average days between host bookings | ≤21 days | >30 days (any host) |
| Guest 90-day repeat rate | ≥20% | <15% |
| NPS score (surveyed monthly) | ≥50 | <40 |
| Churn intervention success rate | ≥50% | <30% |
| Host referral program participation | ≥15% of active hosts | <10% |

### How to Calculate Intervention Success Rate

Of all hosts who were in "at-risk" status (21+ days no booking) and received Level 2 or 3 intervention:
- How many had a booking within 30 days of intervention?
- Success rate = bookings within 30 days / total interventions

A low success rate signals that either (a) the intervention scripts need revision, or (b) the hosts are leaving for reasons the intervention doesn't address (usually: property quality issues or pricing that cannot be fixed by a phone call alone).

## Real-World Scenarios

### Scenario A: The Host Who Got Spooked by a Bad Guest
A host had a difficult guest experience in Month 1 — the guest left the apartment messy and filed a dubious complaint. The host received a partial payout reduction as part of the (incorrect) dispute resolution. In Month 2, the host hasn't listed any new availability.

**Retention response**:
- Day 22 (no new bookings): Host Success calls
- Host explains what happened — clearly upset about the dispute outcome
- Host Success lead reviews the case: was the dispute resolution correct? If not, correct it and issue the missing payout.
- Trust & Safety verifies the guest was in fact a problematic actor
- Host Success messages the host the corrected resolution + the outcome for the guest's account
- Offer: "We want to make this right. We're removing your commission on your next 2 bookings."
- Result: Host relists with updated photos and renewed pricing confidence

**Lesson**: Many host churn cases are not about "not enough bookings" — they're about a specific bad experience that the platform failed to resolve fairly. Fix the injustice first.

### Scenario B: The Guest Who Wants a Discount Every Time
A guest who has completed 4 bookings asks for a discount on every booking via WhatsApp before confirming. "Give me a discount and I'll book."

**Correct response**:
- This guest's behavior is negotiation-seeking, not loyalty-seeking. Yield to it every time and you establish a norm of negotiating every booking.
- Apply the loyalty discount (EGP 150 credit for 3rd booking) if applicable — this is pre-set and transparent.
- If the guest continues: "Our pricing is set by the host and reflects market rates. We do offer loyalty credits for repeat guests — you have [X] credit available on this booking."
- Do NOT create custom discounts outside the program. This creates expectation inflation and signals that asking = discount.

### Scenario C: The Host Who Has Too Many Bookings
A host is fully booked for 3 months straight — excellent occupancy. At Month 3, they message: "I need a break — I'm blocking the calendar for 30 days."

**Response**:
- This is a healthy sign (high occupancy) but creates a churn risk (30-day block often extends).
- Personal message: "That's great to hear! You've been one of our most successful hosts. A break sounds well-earned. When you're ready to reopen, we'd love to prioritize your listing in our upcoming GCC travel campaign — just let us know."
- Keep them engaged during the block: monthly market update, invite to the virtual host community.
- Re-engagement outreach at Day 25: "Hope you're enjoying your break — your listing is ready to go whenever you are. The market's looking strong for [next month]."

## Decision Tree: Retention Action

```
Host has had no booking in 21 days?
  YES → Level 2 intervention. Personal call within 24 hours.
         Investigate: calendar? pricing? listing quality?
         Take action in the call.

Host has had no booking in 30 days and Level 2 was attempted?
  YES → Level 3. Escalate to Host Success Lead.
         Offer: commission waiver, listing refresh, personal property visit.

Host expressed intent to leave?
  YES → Escalate to Founder (≥3 props) or Lead (1–2 props) within 1 hour.
         "What would need to be true for you to stay?"

Guest has not rebooked in 45 days?
  YES → Win-back message with discount code.

Guest's last review was ≤3 stars?
  YES → Personal call within 24 hours. Understand root cause. Recovery offer.

Guest started a booking and didn't complete?
  YES → Abandonment follow-up within 24 hours.
         "Did you find what you were looking for? Can I help find the right property?"
```

## Best Practices

1. **Retention begins at activation.** The highest-leverage retention action is ensuring a host gets their first booking within 21 days and a guest has an excellent first stay. The onboarding experience sets the trajectory for the entire relationship.

2. **Personalize retention outreach.** A WhatsApp message that says "مرحبا عميلنا العزيز" (Dear valued customer) is spam. A message that says "مرحبا [Name]، شفنا إن الوحدة في [neighborhood] معتليهاش حجز من [X] أيام..." is a conversation.

3. **Fix the root cause, not just the symptom.** A host who is churning because their property hasn't been booked in 30 days needs a pricing, listing, or photography fix — not just encouragement. Retention without solving the underlying problem buys 2 more weeks, not 12 more months.

4. **Track churn by cohort.** Hosts who joined in Month 1 vs. Month 6 may churn for different reasons (early adopters were more motivated; later adopters may have had higher expectations). Understanding churn by cohort reveals when in the lifecycle each intervention should happen.

5. **Make the referral program easy to use.** A referral program that requires filling out a form is not a referral program. A WhatsApp message with a link that says "شارك الرابط ده مع أي حد عنده شقة" is a referral program.

## Common Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Treating all churn as a marketing problem**
"We need to run a discount campaign to bring hosts back." If hosts are leaving because dispute resolution is unfair or payment timing is unclear, no discount will fix that. Diagnose the root cause before prescribing the solution.

**Mistake 2: Only tracking aggregate retention metrics**
"Our host retention is 85% — that's fine." But if 10 specific hosts each own 3 properties and 8 of them are churning, that's 24 properties leaving while aggregate retention looks OK. Track retention at the property unit level, not just the host account level.

**Mistake 3: Win-back offers that signal desperation**
"Please come back! Here's 30% off!" after a guest had a bad experience signals that the underlying problem wasn't fixed. A win-back offer should be accompanied by a demonstration that the specific problem is resolved.

**Mistake 4: Letting the Level 2 call become a routine check-in**
The 21-day call must be diagnostic, not social. An agent who calls and says "just checking in, hope everything's good!" without investigating the no-booking situation has wasted the intervention window.

## FAQs

**Q: What's the maximum commission waiver we can offer to retain a host?**
A: Two bookings at 0% commission is the pre-approved maximum for Support Leads and Host Success Leads. Four bookings or cash payments require Founder approval.

**Q: Should we retain every host who wants to leave?**
A: No. Hosts with below-3.5 average review scores should not be retained at all costs — retaining low-quality hosts harms the guest experience and ultimately destroys the marketplace quality. Retention efforts should focus on quality hosts.

**Q: How do we handle a guest who leaves a bad review and then asks for a discount on their next booking?**
A: Separate the two. The review stands unless it violates review integrity policy (Trust & Safety call). The discount request is evaluated on its merits using the loyalty program — not granted as quid pro quo for removing a bad review.
