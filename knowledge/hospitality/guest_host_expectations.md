## Purpose

This article defines what guests are entitled to expect from a StayOS property, what hosts are entitled to expect from guests, and where StayOS draws the line when expectations are not met. Clear expectations reduce disputes, increase satisfaction, and protect both sides from misunderstandings rooted in Egypt's diverse guest and host demographics.

---

## Background

The Cairo accommodation market brings together guests and hosts from dramatically different backgrounds: Egyptian families, solo Egyptian business travelers, GCC vacationers with high service standards, international professionals, and budget travelers. Hosts range from individual apartment owners who have never rented before to experienced property management companies.

Without explicit expectations, both sides import assumptions from other contexts — some guests expect hotel-level service from a private apartment; some hosts treat the apartment as a bedroom they're renting to a friend. Misaligned expectations are the root cause of 60%+ of accommodation disputes.

StayOS's job is to set expectations clearly for both sides and enforce them consistently.

## Core Concept: The Expectations Framework

```
What guests are guaranteed (non-negotiable)
         ↕
What hosts are guaranteed (non-negotiable)
         ↕
What is agreed per listing (variable, disclosed upfront)
         ↕
What is expected but not guaranteed (hospitality baseline)
```

## Part 1: What Guests Are Guaranteed

These guarantees apply to every StayOS booking, regardless of price tier or host.

### Guarantee 1: The Property Is Real and Available

The property physically exists, is located at the stated address, and is available on the booked dates. Any deviation — ghost listing, wrong address, property unavailable at check-in — triggers an immediate full refund plus emergency accommodation if needed.

**How it's enforced**: Property verification at listing approval (physical visit for Tier 2+), identity verification of the host, and the Zero-Ghost Protocol from the Trust Framework.

### Guarantee 2: The Property Matches Its Listing

The bedroom count, bathroom count, photos, and listed amenities must accurately reflect the physical property. Material discrepancies (missing bedroom, wrong neighborhood, significantly worse condition than photos) are "not as described" violations.

**Tolerance**: Minor wear that isn't visible in photos (scuff on a wall, slightly older appliance) is not a violation. A missing bedroom is. The standard: would a reasonable guest feel misled?

**How it's enforced**: Inspection photos captured at listing, pre-cleaning photos per stay. Guests can report within 24 hours of check-in.

### Guarantee 3: The Property Is Safe

The property has functional locks, working electricity, working water, and no known safety hazards. The fire escape is accessible. Balconies have railings. Gas connections are safe.

**How it's enforced**: Physical inspection checklist at listing approval. If a safety issue is discovered during a stay, StayOS must resolve it or relocate the guest at StayOS/host cost.

### Guarantee 4: The Property Is Clean at Check-In

The property has been professionally cleaned since the previous guest's departure. Fresh linens and towels are provided. All dishes are clean and stored. Trash is empty.

**How it's enforced**: Turnover protocol with photo documentation (BR-OPS-03). Cleaning failure is a HIGH-severity issue triggering immediate response.

### Guarantee 5: Privacy and Security

Hosts may not enter the property during a guest's stay without advance notice and guest consent (except for genuine emergencies with immediate notification). Hosts may not install hidden cameras anywhere a guest has a reasonable expectation of privacy (bedrooms, bathrooms). Property access is the guest's alone during the stay.

**How it's enforced**: Listing terms, KYC of hosts, and zero-tolerance policy (permanent ban for camera violations).

### Guarantee 6: Support Is Available

Support is available 8am–10pm daily, with on-call emergency support overnight for CRITICAL issues. Guests always have a WhatsApp number to reach StayOS, and it will be answered within SLA.

## Part 2: What Hosts Are Guaranteed

These guarantees apply to every StayOS host for every booking.

### Guarantee 1: Guest Identity Is Verified

Every guest has undergone identity verification before booking (BR-ID-01). The host is never letting a stranger into their property — they're letting a verified, identified individual with a traceable identity.

**Implication**: If a guest causes damage, StayOS can provide the guest's verified identity information to law enforcement if required.

### Guarantee 2: Payment Is Secured

The guest's payment is collected and held in escrow before the guest ever arrives. The host's payout is guaranteed once the 24-hour dispute window closes (BR-FIN-01). No guest can "forget to pay" or "disappear after checkout."

### Guarantee 3: Dispute Support

If a guest causes damage, violates house rules, or engages in misconduct, the host has StayOS's Trust & Safety team as their advocate. Hosts can document issues and file complaints that will be investigated.

**Limitation**: Hosts must provide evidence (pre/post photos). Verbal claims without evidence are insufficient for financial claims against guests.

### Guarantee 4: Listing Control

Hosts control their listing: they set the price, the rules, the availability, the minimum stay. StayOS does not override host pricing without consent. If platform-suggested pricing conflicts with host preferences, the host's price setting is final.

### Guarantee 5: Review System Is Fair

Guests who leave retaliatory reviews (reviews that are factually inaccurate and appear designed to harm the host after a dispute) can be reported. Trust & Safety investigates and can remove reviews that violate the review integrity policy.

## Part 3: Per-Listing Variable Expectations

These are disclosed upfront by the host and agreed to by the guest at booking. Neither side can claim surprise.

### House Rules

Hosts set house rules that are displayed on the listing and must be accepted before booking:

**Common house rules that are PERMITTED**:
- No smoking (any property)
- No pets
- No parties/events
- Quiet hours (specific times)
- No unregistered additional guests
- Check-in/checkout time window
- Specific area restrictions (no guests in host's private room if shared space)

**House rules that are NOT PERMITTED**:
- Rules that discriminate based on nationality, religion, gender (beyond legal cultural accommodation categories)
- Rules that violate Egyptian consumer protection law
- Rules that would make the property unusable for its stated purpose

### Cultural Accommodation Categories

StayOS permits specific cultural category flags that filter guest eligibility. These exist to match supply and demand in a market where cultural fit drives satisfaction for both sides:

- **Family-Only**: Property is available only to bookings with at least one adult and one minor, or multi-generational groups. Single adults or couples without children are not eligible.
- **Women-Only**: Property is available only to bookings by women. This exists primarily for female solo travelers who prefer this designation.
- **Halal-Certified**: Property confirms no alcohol is served or stored, and no activities incompatible with Islamic values occur on premises.
- **GCC-Ready**: Property meets amenity standards preferred by GCC guests (specific bedding quality, blackout curtains, bidet facilities, Arabic satellite TV, higher cleanliness standard).

**Important**: These categories allow hosts to set their own cultural parameters within legal bounds. They are NOT a basis for StayOS to discriminate — they are disclosed attributes that guests opt into when selecting properties.

## Part 4: The Hospitality Baseline

These are not guaranteed in writing but are expected as a baseline of hospitality:

**For hosts**:
- Respond to guest messages within 2 hours during operational hours
- Provide accurate, complete check-in instructions proactively (don't wait for the guest to ask)
- Leave the property in a condition that respects the next guest (no personal items left)
- Be reachable on the day of check-in

**For guests**:
- Treat the property with the care of a temporary home (not a hotel that will be cleaned behind you)
- Report issues during the stay, not post-checkout
- Respect quiet hours and house rules stated in the listing
- Check out on time or communicate in advance if circumstances require a change

StayOS does not enforce the baseline by taking financial action — these are expectations of a civilized marketplace participant. Repeated violations by either side lead to lower ratings, and sufficiently low ratings lead to removal from the platform.

## Part 5: Egyptian and GCC Guest-Specific Context

Egypt's market has specific dynamics that affect expectations on both sides:

### Egyptian Guest Expectations
- **Price negotiation**: Egyptian guests will sometimes negotiate the listed price. StayOS does not support price negotiation after booking — the listed price is the price. Hosts should set their price for what it's worth.
- **Family group dynamics**: Egyptian family bookings often include extended family members beyond what was listed. Hosts should clarify in their house rules the maximum occupancy.
- **Late check-in acceptance**: Egyptian timing culture often results in arrivals 1–2 hours after the stated check-in time without advance notice. Hosts should be aware of this and not penalize guests for it without communication.
- **Cash preference**: Some Egyptian guests prefer cash transactions and will ask to pay the host directly. This is prohibited on StayOS — all bookings must go through the platform.

### GCC Guest Expectations
- **Service standard expectation**: GCC travelers (particularly from Saudi Arabia, UAE, Kuwait) are accustomed to 4–5 star hotel standards. A Comfort or Premium tier property for a GCC guest is competing with a hotel room in their mental model.
- **Privacy emphasis**: Privacy is critically important for GCC family groups. Host transparency about any shared spaces or neighboring units is essential.
- **Communication style**: GCC guests tend to communicate formally. Use proper formal Arabic (فصحى) in written communication.
- **Documentation needs**: GCC visitors often need documentation for expense claims or visa extensions. Provide receipts and confirmation documents promptly upon request.
- **Ramadan impact**: During Ramadan, GCC travelers staying in Egypt will expect access to restaurants open for suhoor, prayer facilities, and a property environment respectful of religious observance.

## Real-World Scenarios

### Scenario A: The GCC Guest Who Expected Hotel Service
A Saudi family books a Premium tier apartment for 7 nights. On day 2, they message: "We need daily cleaning and fresh towels — that's what we always get at hotels."

**Correct response**:
- Pull the listing: daily cleaning is NOT listed as an included amenity.
- Explain professionally: "Our [property name] does not include daily cleaning in the booking. This is an apartment rental, not a hotel. However, we can arrange a mid-stay cleaning for [EGP amount] if you'd like."
- If the guest says this should have been disclosed: check if the listing is accurate. If the listing implied hotel-level service, that's a listing accuracy issue to fix.
- Lesson: GCC-Ready properties should explicitly state what cleaning services are included.

### Scenario B: The Undeclared Guests
A guest books for 2 people (himself and his wife). Day 2, the host WhatsApps: "I came to drop off extra keys and there were 6 people in the apartment."

**Correct response**:
- Support contacts the guest immediately: "Your booking was for 2 guests. We understand circumstances can change. Can you confirm the number of guests currently in the property?"
- If the guest confirms 6 guests: This is a house rules violation. Per the booking terms, the maximum occupancy is 2. The guest can either: (a) book additional rooms for the extra guests, or (b) the extra guests must leave the property.
- StayOS charges the host's standard per-additional-guest fee if the house rules include one, or takes no financial action if no such policy is in the listing — but documents the violation on the guest's account.

### Scenario C: The Host Who Wanted to Charge More Mid-Stay
Host messages StayOS after a guest checks in: "I want to charge them extra for using the washing machine — it's not in the listing but I should have charged more."

**Correct response**:
- "The guest's booking is confirmed at the listed price. No additional charges can be collected from the guest outside of the booking platform for items not listed at the time of booking. If you'd like to offer washing machine access as an add-on for future bookings, we can include that in your listing."
- The host cannot extract additional money from a confirmed guest. Period.

## Decision Tree: Expectation Violation

```
Guest reports a violation of their guaranteed expectations.

Is it a property access failure (wrong address, non-existent, can't get in)?
  → CRITICAL. Full refund + emergency accommodation. Immediate on-call.

Is it a major amenity missing that was listed (bedroom, AC in claimed AC property)?
  → HIGH. Evidence collection. 24-hour dispute window applies.
  → Full refund likely if not resolvable within 4 hours.

Is it a cleanliness failure?
  → HIGH. Dispatch cleaning team immediately.
  → If not fixable same day: relocation or partial refund.

Is it a host house rule violation (host entering without consent)?
  → HIGH to CRITICAL depending on privacy breach.
  → Trust & Safety investigation. Host account review.

Is it a guest expectation mismatch (expected hotel service, didn't get it)?
  → Check listing accuracy. If listing was accurate, explain the listing terms.
  → No refund for services not listed.
  → Consider goodwill credit if guest experience was genuinely poor.
```

## Best Practices

1. **Listing accuracy is the best dispute prevention.** Every property description should be written by asking: "Will a guest be surprised by anything in this property that isn't in this listing?" If yes, add it.

2. **Communicate expectations proactively to new hosts.** In the first 2 weeks of a new host's listing, StayOS's Host Success team should walk them through what guests will expect. Don't wait for a guest complaint to reveal what the host didn't know.

3. **For GCC properties, audit against the GCC-Ready checklist.** If a property is listed as GCC-Ready, it must actually meet the standards. A Saudi family that arrives to a property that doesn't meet those standards will not return and will leave a review that damages the listing.

4. **House rules must be enforced, or they mean nothing.** A host who has a "no parties" rule but doesn't enforce it for the first guest who has a party has effectively removed that rule. If a rule is in the listing, StayOS supports enforcement.

5. **Document all expectation-setting conversations.** If a host is told "guests will expect X" and later claims they didn't know, the documentation is the defense. Note every onboarding conversation.

## Common Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Not disclosing known property limitations**
A host knows the elevator in the building doesn't work. They don't mention it in the listing. A guest with mobility limitations books the 5th-floor apartment. This is a material omission, not just an inconvenience.

**Mistake 2: Setting expectations verbally but not in the listing**
Host tells a guest during check-in: "The WiFi is a bit slow." The listing says "high-speed WiFi." The verbal disclosure at check-in doesn't override the written listing expectation. The listing must be accurate.

**Mistake 3: Treating all guests as if they're Egyptian**
GCC guests, international guests, and Egyptian guests have genuinely different expectations. A Support agent who responds to a Saudi guest's formal Arabic with Egyptian colloquial Arabic is creating a tone mismatch that signals low quality.

## FAQs

**Q: Can a host reject a guest after the booking is confirmed?**
A: No. Once a booking is confirmed, the host cannot reject the guest. If the host cancels a confirmed booking, they receive no payout, incur a cancellation penalty, and may be suspended. The only exception is if StayOS's Trust & Safety determines a guest poses a safety risk (active fraud investigation, for example).

**Q: Can a guest book without accepting house rules?**
A: No. House rules are displayed and must be accepted as part of the booking flow. A guest who violates rules they accepted is in breach of booking terms, which supports enforcement action by StayOS.

**Q: What if a guest's expectation was based on a review, not the listing?**
A: Reviews are user-generated content and represent individual experiences. They are not StayOS's guarantees. The listing itself is the source of guaranteed expectations.
