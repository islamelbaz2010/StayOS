## Purpose

This article defines what a StayOS-quality property looks like, why these standards exist, and how they are verified and enforced. Every host-facing team member and every field staff member must know these standards before working with a single host.

---

## Background

The Egyptian accommodation market has a pervasive quality problem: listings on OLX, WhatsApp groups, and even international OTAs routinely show professional photography of properties that bear no resemblance to the actual unit. Guests arrive to find dirty, deteriorated, or simply non-existent properties. The resulting trust deficit is the single biggest structural weakness of the Egyptian accommodation market — and the biggest opportunity for StayOS.

StayOS's quality standards are not bureaucratic checkboxes. They are the physical manifestation of the brand promise: "What you see is what you get, or we give you your money back and find you somewhere else."

**Why strict standards in Stage 1 are especially important**: The first 50 hosts set the culture for the next 5,000. Every exception made for a host whose property does not meet standards signals to other hosts that standards are negotiable. They are not.

---

## Core Concept: The Three-Gate Quality System

Every StayOS listing must pass through three gates before going live:

```
Gate 1: Documentation (photos, ID, ownership proof)
Gate 2: Physical Verification (in-person or video inspection)
Gate 3: Guest-Ready Check (the day before first booking)
```

A listing that passes Gates 1 and 2 but fails Gate 3 stays offline until the specific issues are resolved.

---

## Detailed Explanation

### Gate 1: Documentation Requirements

**Host Identity Verification**
- Egyptian national: National ID (رقم قومي) — front and back, verified against government database
- Non-Egyptian resident: Passport + Egyptian residency permit
- Corporate host (company): Commercial register + authorized signatory ID
- All IDs verified against the listing's ownership documentation before any listing is created

**Property Ownership/Authorization Documentation**
- Owned property: Original ownership deed or utility bill in host's name
- Rented property: Lease agreement + written landlord authorization to sublease
- Managed property: Property management agreement with authorization scope clearly defined
- Corporate portfolio: Master management agreement covering all units

**Why ownership documentation matters**: Without it, StayOS unknowingly lists unauthorized sublets, which expose guests to eviction risk and expose StayOS to legal liability. This is non-negotiable.

---

### Gate 2: Physical Verification Standards

StayOS conducts either an in-person inspection (preferred) or a live video walkthrough (accepted for properties >50km from Cairo team base) before any listing goes live.

**Structural Requirements**
- Functioning entrance lock with key, code, or smart-lock access
- All windows and exterior doors close and lock properly
- No visible structural damage (cracks in walls, water damage, mold)
- Working electricity throughout the unit (no missing fixtures, no exposed wiring)
- Working plumbing (hot water, no leaks, functioning toilet)
- Working air conditioning (required for Egypt — not optional)
- Functioning elevator access for units above 3rd floor

**Cleanliness Standard at Verification**
Properties are inspected at their "normal baseline" state, not a staged state. If a property has not been recently cleaned, the inspection is rescheduled (not failed). However, any property with evidence of pest infestation, water damage, or embedded odor fails verification and must remediate before re-inspection.

**Safety Requirements**
- Fire extinguisher in kitchen area (minimum 1kg ABC class)
- Smoke detector installed and functioning (test at inspection)
- Emergency exit clearly accessible
- If building is >4 stories: building must have functioning fire escape routes
- Family property (listing shows children welcome): additional safety check (no uncovered electrical outlets, no sharp furniture edges at child height, window guards if above ground floor)

**Photography Standards**
StayOS provides photography or reviews host photography against these standards:
- All photos taken in natural daylight (or professional lighting — no yellow tint)
- Every room must be photographed (bedroom, bathroom, kitchen/living area, entrance)
- Exterior and building entrance photographed
- No digital alterations beyond color correction (no removal of furniture, no AI-generated additions)
- Primary photo must be the most representative view (not the best angle that hides problems)
- Minimum 8 photos, recommended 15–20

**Why photography standards matter**: The leading cause of guest disputes across all accommodation platforms is "property not as described in photos." StayOS's goal is for a guest who books from photos to be pleasantly surprised — never disappointed.

---

### Gate 3: Guest-Ready Check (Pre-First-Booking)

24–48 hours before the first guest arrival at any property:

**Physical Readiness**
- [ ] Unit cleaned to StayOS standard (see `knowledge/hospitality/turnover_operations.md`)
- [ ] All amenities listed in the listing are physically present and functional
- [ ] Fresh linens and towels set
- [ ] Toiletries (soap, shampoo, toilet paper — minimum 2 rolls) stocked
- [ ] Kitchen: dishes, cups, cutlery present and clean
- [ ] All keys/codes tested and working from outside the building
- [ ] AC units set to cooling mode and tested
- [ ] TV remote with batteries, internet password posted visibly

**Information Readiness**
- [ ] Welcome guide in place (physical or digital) in Arabic and English
- [ ] Emergency contacts posted: local police (122), ambulance (123), StayOS support WhatsApp
- [ ] Building access instructions documented and tested
- [ ] Parking instructions if applicable
- [ ] Nearby pharmacy, grocery, and restaurant information

**Host Communication Readiness**
- [ ] Host confirmed they will be reachable on check-in day
- [ ] Backup contact (building manager or neighbor) provided to StayOS
- [ ] Guest's arrival time communicated to host

## Property Category Standards

StayOS properties are categorized by tier. Each tier has a minimum standard. Properties are categorized by StayOS based on inspection results, not host self-declaration.

### Essential (Budget Tier)
**Target**: Egyptian domestic traveler, budget-conscious, functional stay
- All Gate 1, 2, 3 requirements above
- Air conditioning: 1 unit per bedroom minimum
- Internet: functional WiFi, minimum 10 Mbps
- Kitchen: functional (microwave or stove, basic utensils)
- Linens: clean, no holes or stains
- Nightly rate target: EGP 300–800

### Comfort (Mid-Tier)
**Target**: Egyptian domestic families, GCC travelers on moderate budget
- All Essential requirements, plus:
- Air conditioning: every room
- Internet: 25+ Mbps
- Kitchen: full kitchen (stove, oven, refrigerator)
- Linens: hotel-grade (white or neutral, pressed)
- Additional: washing machine or laundry access
- Dining table for group meals
- Nightly rate target: EGP 800–2,000

### Premium (Upper Tier)
**Target**: GCC families, Egyptian high-income travelers, corporate guests
- All Comfort requirements, plus:
- Internet: 50+ Mbps with backup (Vodafone pocket/Etisalat stick)
- Linens: luxury grade, daily change available
- Kitchen: fully equipped including dishwasher
- Additional amenities: coffee machine, smart TV, blackout curtains
- Security: building security or concierge
- Parking: dedicated parking space
- Views, finishings, and furniture matching the listed photos exactly
- Nightly rate target: EGP 2,000–6,000

### Cultural Category Flags
Properties may additionally carry cultural category flags that carry their own requirements:

**Family-Only**: No unrelated mixed-gender groups accepted. Host must confirm family composition of each group before check-in.

**Halal-Certified**: No alcohol stored or served on premises. No non-halal food prepared in the kitchen by the host. Host must confirm this practice.

**Women-Only**: Only female guests or female-led family groups. Male visitors not permitted in the property.

**GCC-Ready**: Arabic check-in materials available, GCC payment methods accepted, Gulf hospitality standards applied (e.g., prayer mat and qibla direction provided, dates and Arabic coffee upon arrival if practical).

## Real-World Scenarios

### Scenario A: The Over-Enthusiastic Host
A host in New Cairo has a beautiful apartment, but their photos were taken 3 years ago before the building installed a new elevator and renovated the lobby. The current photos show an old, dimly lit entrance. The unit itself is excellent.

**Correct action**: Re-photograph the entrance and building. Do not publish with old photos. The old photos will disappoint guests even though the property is actually better — they'll arrive expecting the worst and may interpret anything as a disappointment.

### Scenario B: The Failed Inspection
A host's property fails Gate 2 because the bathroom has a persistent leak and the kitchen stove burner is broken.

**Correct action**: Issue a specific remediation list with photos of the issues. Set a 14-day remediation deadline. Offer to connect the host with a trusted plumber and handyman from StayOS's partner network. Re-inspect after remediation is claimed complete. Do not publish until Gate 2 is passed — no exceptions.

### Scenario C: The Star Misrepresentation
A host claims their apartment is "5-star quality" and wants to be listed in the Premium tier. Inspection reveals standard mid-tier finishings, no dishwasher, and internet at 20 Mbps.

**Correct action**: List as Comfort tier. Explain the specific gaps to reach Premium. If the host disagrees, remind them that tier is determined by StayOS inspection — guests compare the listing to the inspection standard, not to the host's self-assessment. Offer to re-inspect if they make the specific upgrades.

## Decision Tree: Property Fails Verification

```
Property fails Gate 2 inspection. What happens next?

Is the failure a safety issue (structural damage, no fire extinguisher, no working lock)?
  YES → Property cannot be listed until safety issue is fully remediated.
        Set 30-day remediation window. Host is responsible for all remediation costs.
        Re-inspection required before listing.
  NO  → Continue below.

Is the failure a cleanliness issue (dirty unit, stained linens, pest evidence)?
  YES → Schedule re-inspection in 7 days after host claims remediation.
        If re-inspection passes, list the property.
        If second failure: 90-day pause on listing, host education call required.
  NO  → Continue below.

Is the failure a documentation issue (missing ownership proof, ID not verified)?
  YES → Send exact document list. Give 14 days to provide documents.
        List blocked until documents verified. No exceptions.
  NO → 
        Record the specific issue and set a remediation path.
        Communicate clearly what must change before the property goes live.
```

## Best Practices

1. **Photograph every inspection.** Use time-stamped, geotagged photos for every gate 2 inspection. These become the evidence base if a dispute arises later about whether the property matched its listing.

2. **Separate inspection from relationship.** The field inspector's job is to pass/fail objectively. The host success manager's job is the relationship. Do not have the same person do both on the same property — it creates pressure to pass properties that should fail.

3. **Update listing photos at every major change.** If a host remodels, re-photographs immediately. Outdated photography that shows a property better than its current state is grounds for immediate dispute and refund.

4. **Reward hosts who exceed standards.** Properties that receive consistently high guest satisfaction scores (≥4.5) should receive visible "StayOS Verified Excellence" badges, priority search placement, and lower commission rates. Quality must pay.

5. **Decline gracefully.** When a property fails and cannot be listed, communicate clearly why, what would change the outcome, and how StayOS can help the host get there. A host who is declined but treated respectfully often comes back 3 months later with a remediated property and becomes a strong advocate.

## Common Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Passing a property "conditionally" (it will be fixed before the first guest)**
This always ends in a guest arriving to a property with the promised fix not done. Either it passes inspection or it doesn't.

**Mistake 2: Taking the host's word on documentation**
"I own it but the deed is at the lawyer" is not ownership verification. Every listing must have documentation before going live. Verbal assurances do not meet the standard.

**Mistake 3: Using photos the host provides without review**
Host-provided photos often include wide-angle lens distortions that make rooms look double their actual size, edited out furniture to make space look larger, or photos from before renovations that made the space worse. Review every photo before publishing.

**Mistake 4: Applying Premium tier standards to hosts who didn't ask for Premium**
Not every host needs to meet Premium standards. A host in the Comfort tier who adds a coffee machine has not become Premium — and should not be penalized for not having a dishwasher. Apply the right tier standards to the right tier listing.

## FAQs

**Q: What happens if a guest arrives and the property doesn't match the listing?**
A: This triggers the dispute resolution protocol. StayOS support is contacted within the first 2 hours. If the property materially misrepresents the listing (different location, significantly worse condition, major amenity missing), the guest is immediately offered: (a) resolution within 4 hours (host fixes the issue) or (b) full refund + alternative accommodation. See `knowledge/trust/dispute_resolution.md`.

**Q: Can a host list multiple properties with a single ID verification?**
A: Yes, if all properties are documented (owned or authorized). Identity verification is per host, not per property. Property documentation is per property.

**Q: What is the fee for inspection?**
A: Inspection is free as part of StayOS onboarding. This is a supply subsidy cost. In the future, re-inspection for remediated properties after a second failure may incur a small administrative fee.

**Q: How long does the verification process take?**
A: Gate 1 (document review): 1–2 business days. Gate 2 (physical inspection): 3–7 days from submission (subject to field staff availability in the area). Gate 3 (pre-first-booking check): conducted 24–48 hours before the first booking. Total: minimum 5 days, typical 7–10 days.
